## What is actually happening

"No Service" and "SOS" do not always mean there is no signal where you are. Most of the time the phone has simply given up looking, or it is holding on to a tower that is too far away. The phone is a radio, and radios get stuck.

So before you drive to town to buy a signal booster, run these in order. Nine times out of ten one of the first three brings the bars back.

## The steps

### Step 1 — Toggle Airplane Mode
![iPhone Control Centre with the Airplane Mode button highlighted](/tech-support/connectivity/fix-no-service-sos-mode-rural-montana/img/step-1.png)
Pull down Control Centre on iPhone (swipe from the top-right corner) or swipe down from the top on Android. Tap the **Airplane** icon so it turns on. Wait a full 15 seconds. Tap it again to turn it off.

This is the single most effective fix. It forces the phone to drop whatever tower it was clinging to and scan for a fresh one.

### Step 2 — Restart the phone
![Power button menu showing Slide to power off](/tech-support/connectivity/fix-no-service-sos-mode-rural-montana/img/step-2.png)
A restart does what Airplane Mode does, but deeper — it also clears the part of the phone that decides which network to use. Hold the power button, slide to power off, wait ten seconds, and turn it back on.

### Step 3 — Make sure Wi-Fi Calling is on
![iPhone Settings showing Wi-Fi Calling enabled](/tech-support/connectivity/fix-no-service-sos-mode-rural-montana/img/step-3.png)
This is the one people miss, and it is the biggest help at a rural address. Wi-Fi Calling sends your calls and texts over your home internet instead of the cell tower.

On iPhone: **Settings > Phone > Wi-Fi Calling**. On Android: **Settings > Network & internet > SIMs > Wi-Fi Calling**. Turn it on.

> [!warn] Wi-Fi Calling needs a 911 address on file with your carrier. Set that up the first time you turn it on — it takes a minute and it matters if you ever have to call for help.

### Step 4 — Check for a carrier settings update
![iPhone Settings, General, About screen](/tech-support/connectivity/fix-no-service-sos-mode-rural-montana/img/step-4.png)
Carriers push small updates that tell the phone how to talk to their towers. On iPhone, open **Settings > General > About** and wait ten seconds — if an update is available, a prompt appears. On Android, check **Settings > System > System update**.

### Step 5 — Try choosing a network by hand
![iPhone Carrier settings list showing available networks](/tech-support/connectivity/fix-no-service-sos-mode-rural-montana/img/step-5.png)
This tells the phone to stop guessing. On iPhone: **Settings > Cellular > Network Selection**, turn off Automatic, and pick your carrier from the list. On Android: **Settings > Network & internet > Internet > your carrier > Network operators**.

If your carrier is not in the list at all, that is a strong sign you are genuinely outside coverage.

### Step 6 — Look for a physical blocker
![A thick phone case with a magnetic mount on the back](/tech-support/connectivity/fix-no-service-sos-mode-rural-montana/img/step-6.png)
A thick metal case, a magnetic car mount, or a wallet case with magnets can weaken the antenna. Take the case off and watch the bars for a minute. It sounds unlikely — it is not.

### Step 7 — Move to a window or upstairs
![Person holding a phone near an upstairs window](/tech-support/connectivity/fix-no-service-sos-mode-rural-montana/img/step-7.png)
Radio waves pass through glass far better than through log walls, metal siding, or a basement floor. If you get signal upstairs but not in the kitchen, that is your answer, and a booster or Wi-Fi Calling is the real fix.

### Step 8 — Check the SIM
![SIM tray being removed with a pin tool](/tech-support/connectivity/fix-no-service-sos-mode-rural-montana/img/step-8.png)
Power the phone off, pop the SIM tray with the pin tool, and look at the gold contact pad. Wipe it with a dry cloth, put it back the same way it came out, and power on. If your phone uses an eSIM, skip this step.

### Step 9 — Report the dead zone
![FCC National Broadband Map on a laptop screen](/tech-support/connectivity/fix-no-service-sos-mode-rural-montana/img/step-9.png)
Carriers plan towers from complaint data. Call your carrier, tell them the address and roughly how many people are affected, and ask them to log it. It will not fix today, but it is how rural coverage actually improves.

## When it is not your phone

If a neighbour on the same carrier has no signal either, it is the network, not you — and no amount of fiddling will help. Your options are Wi-Fi Calling, a signal booster aimed at the nearest tower, or a satellite-based service. That last one is covered in [the satellite internet guide](/tech-support/connectivity/satellite-internet-slow-starlink-hughesnet/).

If your signal is fine but the internet at home is the problem, go to [boosting Wi-Fi across a large property](/tech-support/connectivity/boost-wifi-signal-large-home-outbuildings/) instead.
