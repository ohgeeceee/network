## Why the TV buffers when the phone does not

A streaming TV or stick is the most demanding thing on your home Wi-Fi, and often the oldest. It sits against a wall, sometimes behind a metal cabinet, and it holds the signal worse than the phone in your pocket.

The fix is usually not a new TV. It is getting a stronger signal to the spot the TV actually sits in — and the most reliable way to do that is a cable.

## The steps

### Step 1 — Restart both ends together
![A router and a streaming stick with power cables](/tech-support/hardware/smart-tv-streaming-weak-wifi/img/step-1.png)
Unplug the router and the TV or stick. Wait a full 30 seconds. Plug the **router in first** and give it a minute, then the TV. Doing them together clears the stale connection between them, which is a very common cause of buffering.

### Step 2 — Read the TV's own signal strength
![A smart TV network settings screen showing signal strength](/tech-support/hardware/smart-tv-streaming-weak-wifi/img/step-2.png)
In the TV's settings, open the network status screen. It will show a signal strength or quality reading. If it says "fair" or "weak", you have your answer — this is a signal problem, not the app.

### Step 3 — Pick the right band for the distance
![A TV Wi-Fi list showing a 2.4 GHz and a 5 GHz network](/tech-support/hardware/smart-tv-streaming-weak-wifi/img/step-3.png)
- TV in the **same room** as the router: use **5 GHz** (the name usually ends in `-5G`). Faster.
- TV **across the house**: use **2.4 GHz**. Slower, but it reaches through more walls.

Pick for distance, not for the number.

### Step 4 — Free the stick from the cabinet
![A streaming stick on a short HDMI extension cable clear of the TV](/tech-support/hardware/smart-tv-streaming-weak-wifi/img/step-4.png)
A streaming stick plugged directly into the back of a TV, inside a metal or glass cabinet, is in the worst possible place for radio. A short HDMI extension cable gets it out into open air. It is a two-dollar fix that often does more than anything else here.

### Step 5 — Run a cable if you can
![An ethernet cable running from a router to a TV](/tech-support/hardware/smart-tv-streaming-weak-wifi/img/step-5.png)
If the TV has an ethernet port, use it. It is faster and it never drops. If there is no port or no easy route, a **powerline adapter** (which sends the internet through your home's electrical wiring) usually beats Wi-Fi and costs far less than a new TV.

### Step 6 — Check the streaming quality setting
![A streaming app's video quality settings screen](/tech-support/hardware/smart-tv-streaming-weak-wifi/img/step-6.png)
Most apps let you cap the picture quality. If your connection is genuinely limited, setting the app to a lower quality stops it fighting for bandwidth it does not have — and a steady 720p beats a 4K that buffers every minute.

> [!warn] If the TV is on satellite internet, remember that evenings are congested for everyone on the link. Buffering between 7pm and 11pm may be the connection, not your Wi-Fi. See [the satellite guide](/tech-support/connectivity/satellite-internet-slow-starlink-hughesnet/).

## The order that saves money

Try the free things first: restart, band choice, move the stick. Then the cheap things: an HDMI extension, an ethernet cable, a powerline adapter. Only then consider a mesh system or a new TV — and by that point you will know exactly which problem you are solving.
